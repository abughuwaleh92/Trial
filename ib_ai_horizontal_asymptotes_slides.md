## Slide 4 — Warm-Up Activity: *Paper to the Moon*
**Prompt:** If paper thickness is about 0.1 mm and we fold/double repeatedly, can it reach the Moon?

- Step-by-step table appears with animation:
  1. Initial thickness: \(0.1\text{ mm}\)
  2. After 1 fold (doubled): \(0.2\text{ mm}\)
  3. After 2 folds: \(0.4\text{ mm}\)
  4. After 3 folds: \(0.8\text{ mm}\)
  5. ...
  6. After 42 folds: \(0.1\times 2^{42}\text{ mm}\)

- Calculation on slide:
  - \(2^{42}=4,398,046,511,104\)
  - Thickness \(=0.1\times 2^{42}\text{ mm}=439,804,651,110.4\text{ mm}\)
  - Convert to km: \(439,804.651\text{ km}\)
  - Average Earth–Moon distance \(\approx 384,400\text{ km}\)
  - **Conclusion:** It goes beyond the Moon.

**Visuals needed:**
- Paper-fold sequence image/icons
- Moon distance image with comparison bar

⏱️ **Watch timer on slide:** 7 minutes

## Slide 7 — Scenario A: Cooling Coffee
- **Image:** steaming coffee cup cooling on a table
- **Model example:** \(T(t)=22+68(0.85)^t\)
- **Graph:** decreasing curve approaching \(y=22\)
- **Questions:**
  1. Is this growth or decay?
  2. What is the horizontal asymptote?
  3. What does \(22\) represent physically?
  4. Can temperature drop below this in this model?

**Interactive element:** Click word **“Graph”** to reveal/overlay graph clearly.

---

## Slide 8 — Scenario B: Drug in Bloodstream
- **Image:** medicine/drip in patient bloodstream
- **Model example:** \(C(t)=50e^{-0.4t}+2\)
- **Graph:** decaying curve approaching \(y=2\)
- **Questions:**
  1. What does asymptote \(y=2\) represent?
  2. Why does the concentration not go below this value in this model?
  3. How fast does the concentration change early vs late?

**Interactive element:** Click **“Graph”** for highlighted graph.

---

## Slide 9 — Scenario C: Charging Battery
- **Image:** phone charging/battery icon
- **Model example:** \(B(t)=100-90e^{-0.6t}\)
- **Graph:** increasing curve approaching \(y=100\)
- **Questions:**
  1. Why does charging slow near full battery?
  2. What is the horizontal asymptote?
  3. Can the model go above 100?

**Interactive element:** Click **“Graph”** to display enlarged graph.

---

## Slide 10 — Scenario D: Saturation Model
- **Image:** adoption/usage saturation chart or habitat limit
- **Model example (logistic):** \(P(t)=\dfrac{5000}{1+24e^{-0.5t}}\)
- **Graph:** S-curve approaching \(y=5000\)
- **Questions:**
  1. What limits growth in this context?
  2. What is the long-term value?
  3. Why is this more realistic than unlimited exponential growth?

**Interactive element:** Click **“Graph”** to focus graph.

## Slide 12 — Concept Construction (Teacher-facilitated inquiry)
- Show dynamic graph with slider parameters (e.g., in Desmos):
  - Form: \(y=a\cdot b^x + k\)
- Ask:
  1. “What changes when we move \(a\)?”
  2. “What changes when we move \(b\)?”
  3. “What changes when we move \(k\)?”
  4. “Which parameter controls the horizontal asymptote?”

**Target understanding:** Asymptote is \(y=k\).

⏱️ **Watch timer on slide:** 6 minutes

---

## Slide 13 — In Pairs Activity
- Students use graphing calculator or Desmos.
- Provide mixed equations; each pair identifies:
  1. Growth or decay
  2. Horizontal asymptote
  3. Possible real-life application

- Add **interactive vocabulary/glossary choices**:
  - limit, approach, asymptote, growth factor, decay factor, saturation

- Add one of these formats (or combine):
  - **Matching cards:** equation ↔ asymptote ↔ application
  - **Arrange pieces:** students place equation next to correct asymptote and context

⏱️ **Watch timer on slide:** 6 minutes

- Prompt them to discuss horizontal vs vertical asymptotes.

- Ensure transitions connect ideas:

- Avoid labeling anything as “trial lesson” in the topic/title.
